An Assumption Log for Comparing Job Offers

CareerRadar Editorial / 2026.10.27

Offer comparison improves when unverified assumptions about compensation, authority, location, and evaluation are recorded separately from confirmed terms.

Evidence and interpretation

CareerRadar separates source-supported facts from interpretation. The World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs Report 2025 emphasizes analytical thinking and growing technology skills while also highlighting resilience, leadership, and social influence. The OECD Employment Outlook 2025 discusses mobility, career guidance, and training as skill needs change. These sources provide context; they do not guarantee an individual compensation outcome.

PMI salary-survey findings should be read as associations rather than proof that a credential causes higher pay. Upwork research is useful as directional evidence about U.S. skilled knowledge work, but it is platform-sponsored and U.S.-specific. CareerRadar uses such evidence to inform hypotheses, then expects the reader to test those hypotheses against current target roles and markets.

CareerRadar action check

Before the next application or interview, ask whether you are separating market value from a particular platform's inventory, whether you can explain outcomes and decisions, whether you are testing more than one channel, whether rejection reasons are being recorded, and whether you have a 30- to 90-day review point.

The objective is not to be selected by one recruiter or rely on one employer brand. The objective is to make your career capital legible enough that multiple routes can create real options.

If a job move is one of your options, do not let one company's response define your market value.
